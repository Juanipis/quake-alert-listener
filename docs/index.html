Home Assistant YAML snippets

Let the bridge push to a webhook the moment an alert lands, or read it as a REST sensor.

POST/api/webhook/quake_alert_local 
# automations.yaml  ·  run the bridge with --webhook-url
- alias: "Earthquake early alert"
  triggers:
    - trigger: webhook
      webhook_id: "quake_alert_local"
      local_only: true
  actions:
    - action: notify.notify
      data:
        title: "Shaking in {{ trigger.json.s_wave_eta_s | int }} s"
        message: "Drop, cover, and hold on."
# configuration.yaml
rest:
  - resource: "http://127.0.0.1:8990/status"
    scan_interval: 10
    sensor:
      - name: "Quake Listener Latency"
        value_template: "{{ value_json.google_mcs.latency_ms }}"
        unit_of_measurement: "ms"

02 / How it works

Push, not polling.

Most smart-home quake integrations ask a public API for news every 30 to 60 seconds. With seismic waves, that's often the whole warning window. This one keeps sockets open to the networks that issue warnings and gets told.

Push feedsocket stays open
on push
0 s30 s60 s

Illustration with one quake at 8 s. A poller only finds out on its next request; a push lands as soon as the network publishes it. For scale: P-waves travel at roughly 6 km/s, the damaging S-waves at about 3.5 km/s. The gap between them is the whole warning window.

03 / Where it stands

Honest status: early warnings where they exist.

Official early warnings reach the bridge in seconds where agencies publish them (Japan, mainland China). A Raspberry Shake gives you on-site P-wave triggers anywhere. EMSC, USGS and SGC report every quake minutes later. Google's Android Earthquake Alerts are monitored via direct MCS socket with S2 Level 8 cell subscriptions (Field 29) and a 22-node fleet; its first catch was Google crisis alerts (hurricanes, US flood warnings), which share the channel and are filtered out of the earthquake path. Intensities follow Allen, Wald & Worden (2012).
